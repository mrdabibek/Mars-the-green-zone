# Mars: Last Green

2026 NASA International Space Apps Challenge · Space Mission Design Game

## Project summary

Mars: Last Green is a playable educational game about planning and operating a Mars surface mission with limited supplies. Students choose equipment within an eight-unit payload budget, restore a damaged outpost, and keep a greenhouse alive while managing power, water, and suit oxygen. Packing spare parts improves repair capacity, while a research drive increases the science score. During the mission, dust storms reduce solar output, and a simplified Sabatier system lets players trade electrical power for greater water recovery. To complete the mission, players preserve viable seeds, restore communications, and transmit their findings. A debrief reports seeds saved, water used, repairs, research status, and elapsed time. The game draws on NASA resources about Mars, controlled crop growth, and space-station life support. By linking planning decisions to visible outcomes, the prototype turns engineering trade-offs into choices students can test and discuss.

## NASA resources

- [Mars Facts](https://science.nasa.gov/mars/facts/) informed the thin atmosphere and pressurized habitat.
- [Mars surface power generation](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/mars-surface-power-generation-challenges-and-considerations.pdf) explains why airborne dust can reduce solar power. The game models that relationship.
- [NASA space crop research](https://www.nasa.gov/science-research/nasa-plant-researchers-explore-question-of-deep-space-food-crops/) informed the greenhouse requirements for controlled conditions.
- [Environmental Control and Life Support Systems](https://www.nasa.gov/reference/environmental-control-and-life-support-systems-eclss/) provides the reference for the simplified Sabatier water recovery mechanic.
- [Blue Marble Next Generation](https://science.nasa.gov/earth/earth-observatory/blue-marble-next-generation/base-topography-bathymetry/) provides the Earth's surface texture in the prologue.

The prototype covers payload planning, Mars surface operations, and mission evaluation. Environment and resource rates are simplified for gameplay. Earth’s destruction and the hostile creatures are fictional. The prototype uses AI-assisted code, 3D assets and synthesized radio dialogue.
